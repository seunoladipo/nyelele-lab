Research

What urban forests give back, and to whom.

We assess how alternative planning and management options change the supply of and demand for multiple ecosystem services — and where those services trade off against each other rather than stack up.

Research themes

Three questions we keep returning to.

Trade-offs and synergies

Management that maximizes one service rarely maximizes them all. We quantify where objectives reinforce one another and where a genuine choice has to be made.

Climate resilience and restoration

Identifying priority areas for forest restoration, and testing which configurations hold up under changing climate conditions.

Benefits and beneficiaries

A socio-ecological lens linking services to the people who actually receive them, including health outcomes and the equity of that distribution.

Approach

Methods on both sides of the interface.

  • Spatial analysisLand cover, canopy, and exposure mapping across cities and watersheds.
  • Ecosystem modellingBiophysical models of service supply under alternative management scenarios.
  • Scenario designPlanning and management options built with, not just for, the places they affect.
  • Social methodsInterviews, focus groups, and surveys that surface demand, priorities, and constraints.
  • Field measurementGround data to calibrate and check what the models claim.
