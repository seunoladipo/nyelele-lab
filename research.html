A place-based social-ecological approach for just climate, biodiversity, and well-being outcomes in the Miombo Woodlands

Nature-based solutions (NbS), including tree plantings, stormwater ponds, bioswales, and green roofs, are being implemented in urban areas to alleviate climate threats such as stream degradation, flood risk, urban heat islands, and water and air pollution. The goal of this project is to assess the effectiveness of different scenarios and configurations of NbS in addressing urban water management, air quality and air temperature challenges in Charlottesville and surrounding urban areas of Albemarle County. Our group is utilizing publicly available models and tools to estimate the air pollutant and air temperature reductions associated with different NbS. We are also using different methods of assessing equity to assess the distribution of ecosystem services and benefits across socio-economic and demographic characteristics of the population.

Blueprints for AI Equity and Transparency in Climate Science

Charity Nyelele and Ava Birdwell

The rapid innovation and deployment of Artificial intelligence (AI) has accelerated advancements to reduce the unequal harms of climate change and provide more equitable benefits from climate solutions. However, there are concerns about how fair, transparent, and accountable AI innovations are. Our project brings together climate scientists, AI experts, climate activists, policymakers, and representatives from affected communities to develop equitable and inclusive engagement on AI transparency in climate science.

Using a Historical Lens to Understand Environmental Justice and Equity Issues in Urban Forestry

Charity Nyelele, Sarah Fong, MaeEllen S. Megginson, Yatri Karki, and Brookelyn Mitchell

While increasing tree cover through tree planting is seen as an obvious solution to many environmental and human health problems, as evidenced by the large-scale tree planting initiatives across multiple United States (US) cities, tree cover distributions and planting locations reveal inequities along socio-economic and demographic lines. The success of these projects has varied greatly across the country. This work seeks to understand factors that influence tree cover distributions and green infrastructure investments in different cities of the US.

Climate Restoration Initiative (CRI)

Charity Nyelele and Pinky Rani Sarkar

Carbon dioxide removal methods, such as forestation, bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS), and direct air carbon capture and storage (DACCS), are serious contenders in discussions about how to fight climate change. However, how, where, and when to use these options effectively is not clear, and research examining both technical and social dimensions of potential deployment remains uncommon. The Environmental Institute-led CRI focuses on specific aspects of carbon reduction in Virginia, including nature-based approaches, technological advances, economic and social issues. Our group explores the potential carbon sequestration and storage benefits of afforestation.

Understanding the Perceived Role of Trees in Urban Spaces

Charity Nyelele and Duha Aykan

Trees provide ecosystem services such as air pollutant removal, carbon storage and sequestration, urban heat island reduction, stormwater runoff reduction as well as other socio-economic benefits linked to improved human health and well-being. Through a series of place-based approaches, this study seeks to improve our understanding of the perceived role of tree cover in providing important ecosystem services and benefits to urban residents.
