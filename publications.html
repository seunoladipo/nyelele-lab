Science of The Total Environment 2025

Valuing co-benefits of forest fuels treatment for reducing wildfire risk in California's Sierra Nevada↗

Han Guo, Michael Goulden, Min Gon Chung, Qingqing Xu, Charity Nyelele, Weichao Guo, Benis Egoh, Martha Conklin, Catherine Keske, Mohammad Safeeq, Roger Bales

Using highly forested land in California's Sierra Nevada as study areas, this study provides a methodology to quantify the economic benefits of forest fuels treatment in mitigating wildfire-induced losses across multiple ecosystem services, including carbon storage, timber provisioning, erosion regulation, and air-quality regulation. Integrating historical data on forest disturbances, ecological variables, and market-based ecosystem service valuation models.

2024
Ecohydrology 2024

Valuation of forest-management and wildfire disturbance on water and carbon fluxes in mountain headwaters↗

Min Gon Chung, Han Guo, Charity Nyelele, Benis N. Egoh, Michael L. Goulden, Catherine M. Keske, Roger C. Bales

This study found that active forest management such as commercial thinning and low severity burns reduces tree density and water consumption, boosting mountain water runoff for up to 15 years. Putting a concrete dollar value on these water gains gives land managers a practical financial roadmap to fund proactive treatments, helping protect overgrown forests from catastrophic wildfires and drought.

A multi-benefit framework for funding forest management in fire-driven ecosystems across the Western US↗

Kimberly Quesnel Seipp, Tessa Maurer, Micah Elias, Phil Saksa, Catherine Keske, Kirsten Oleson, Benis Egoh, Rachael Cleveland, Charity Nyelele, Nicolas Goncalves, Kyle Hemes, Peter Wyrsch, David Lewis, Min Gon Chung, Han Guo, Martha Conklin, Roger Bales

This paper proposes a multi-benefit, collaborative framework to accelerate forest management in the Western U.S. by quantifying diverse ecosystem services to engage public and private beneficiaries in cost-sharing.

Ecological Indicators 2023

Using social media data and machine learning to map recreational ecosystem services↗

Charity Nyelele, Catherine Keske, Min Gon Chung, Han Guo, Benis N. Egoh

This study demonstrates how geotagged photos from social media (Flickr) can be used to estimate travel costs and fill data gaps in valuing recreational ecosystem services in California's Tahoe Central Sierra Initiative area. When combined with official survey data, this crowdsourced approach scaled estimated annual recreational travel costs in the region to between $1.35 billion and $1.84 billion.

Science of The Total Environment 2023

Valuing the benefits of forest restoration on enhancing hydropower and water supply in California's Sierra Nevada↗

Han Guo, Michael Goulden, Min Gon Chung, Charity Nyelele, Benis Egoh, Catherine Keske, Martha Conklin, Roger Bales

Strategically planned fuels-reduction treatments can deliver water-related benefits of over $3,000 per hectare — enough that water-supply and hydropower gains can offset the cost of forest restoration outright. Drier years yield greater revenue per area treated, strengthening the case for partnerships between forest managers and water and hydropower providers.

Ecosystem Services 2022

A review of machine learning and big data applications in addressing ecosystem service research gaps↗

Kyle Manley, Charity Nyelele, Benis N. Egoh

Ecosystem service research is limited by three persistent problems: patchy data, poorly quantified uncertainty, and weak links between social and ecological systems. This review argues that big data can fill data gaps directly while machine learning handles the nonlinear socio-ecological relationships that simpler proxies flatten — and identifies where implementation still lags the opportunity.

Urban Forestry & Urban Greening 2022

A comparison of tree planting prioritization frameworks: i-Tree Landscape versus spatial decision support tool↗

Charity Nyelele, Charles N. Kroll, David J. Nowak

This study compared tree planting prioritization scenarios identified from i-Tree Landscape’s priority planting index to optimal scenarios identified from a spatially explicit multi-objective decision support framework at the census block group level in the Bronx, NY. It also explored four scenarios with varying objectives considering populations below the poverty line, avoided runoff, and PM2.5 air pollutant removal monetary benefits

PLOS ONE 2021

Rewilding and restoring nature in a changing world↗

Benis N. Egoh, Charity Nyelele, Karen D. Holl, James M. Bullock, Steve Carver, Christopher J. Sandom

An introduction to a PLOS ONE collection on rewilding and restoration, setting out four threads that run through the field: the growing recognition of restoration under rapid environmental change, how benefits and beneficiaries shape financing, the role of the largest plants and animals in driving biosphere-scale processes, and what rewilding has achieved in the developing world.

The equity of urban forest ecosystem services and benefits in the Bronx, NY↗

Charity Nyelele, Charles N. Kroll

This paper asks whether the benefits of urban trees — carbon storage and sequestration, PM2.5 removal, heat index reduction, and stormwater runoff control — are distributed unevenly across the Bronx with respect to income, race, population density, poverty, and educational attainment.

2019
Urban Forestry & Urban Greening 2019

Present and future ecosystem services of trees in the Bronx, NY↗

Charity Nyelele, Charles N. Kroll, David J. Nowak

Using a spatially distributed implementation of the i-Tree model suite, this study estimates the ecosystem services delivered by a recent tree planting initiative in each census block group of the Bronx, comparing 2010 against three 2030 canopy scenarios that differ in assumed tree mortality. Trees in the Bronx were valued at $37.6 million in 2010, rising to $40.7–43.9 million by 2030 if existing canopy is maintained and new plantings reach maturity.
