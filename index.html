About the lab

Bridging the social and ecological sciences.

The research group led by Dr. Charity Nyelele in the Department of Environmental Sciences at the University of Virginia. Our research is centered on bridging the gap between the social and ecological sciences, with the aim to improve our understanding of issues at the nature-people interface, particularly how nature delivers ecosystem services and benefits to humans and how to limit human impacts on the environment. We are interested broadly in the links between biodiversity, climate change and ecosystem services and benefits. Our current research is primarily on forested systems, especially in urban areas, to assess the impacts of alternative planning and management options on the demand and supply of multiple ecosystem services (including stormwater management, air quality improvements, air temperature reductions and carbon storage and sequestration) as well as trade-offs and synergies among these ecosystem services. We are also interested in exploring issues around climate resilience and the identification of priority areas for forest restoration. We use a socio-ecological lens to link the ecosystem services and benefits, including health outcomes to beneficiaries. The goal of our work is to inform the development of locally tailored strategies and forest management efforts that address various climate, environmental, and societal concerns. .
