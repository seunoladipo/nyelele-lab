Research

What forests give back, and to whom.

We assess how alternative planning and management options change the supply of and demand for multiple ecosystem services — and where those services trade off against each other rather than accumulate.

Themes

Three questions we keep returning to

Theme 01

Trade-offs and synergies

Management that maximizes one service rarely maximizes them all. We quantify where objectives reinforce one another and where a genuine choice has to be made.

Theme 02

Climate resilience and restoration

Identifying priority areas for forest restoration, and testing which configurations hold up under changing climate conditions.

Theme 03

Benefits and beneficiaries

A socio-ecological lens linking services to the people who actually receive them, including health outcomes and the equity of that distribution.

Services

What we measure

  • Stormwater
    Interception and infiltration that reduce runoff volume and flooding risk.
  • Air quality
    Pollutant removal by canopy, and the populations living downwind of it.
  • Temperature
    Shade and evapotranspiration set against urban heat exposure.
  • Carbon
    Standing stocks and annual sequestration across the urban forest.
Approach

Methods on both sides of the interface

  • Spatial analysis
    Land cover, canopy, and exposure mapping across cities and watersheds.
  • Ecosystem modelling
    Biophysical models of service supply under alternative management scenarios.
  • Scenario design
    Planning options built with, not merely for, the places they affect.
  • Social methods
    Interviews, focus groups, and surveys that surface demand, priorities, and constraints.
  • Field measurement
    Ground data to calibrate and check what the models claim.
